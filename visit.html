Practical information

Plan Your Visit

Kokino is about 30 km north-east of Kumanovo and roughly two hours from Skopje. Here is how to get there, what to bring, and how long to allow.

Time on site

Allow 1.5 to 2 hours to see the site properly, plus about 15 minutes to climb from the car park to the summit.

Warden on duty

A warden service is present from 9:00 to 17:00. They can help you find your way around the site and supply brochures, leaflets and other printed material.

The climb

The trail rises from roughly 920 m at the parking area to just over 1,000 m at the summit — a walk of about 20 minutes on a gentle, sunlit southern slope.

Getting there

By car

Kokino lies in the north-eastern part of North Macedonia, about 30 km north-east of Kumanovo. From Kumanovo the road passes the village of Staro Nagorichane and runs through Dragomance. After that village a narrow asphalt road leads to the foot of the volcanic hill known as Tatichev Kamen, on whose summit the site stands.

Work on protecting and presenting the site is still ongoing, so for now vehicles park at the roadside at the foot of the hill.

Driving from Kumanovo you will pass through Staro Nagorichane. It is worth stopping in the village centre for the church of St George, famous for its 14th-century frescoes — among the finest examples of Byzantine painting of that period anywhere.

By bicycle

From Kumanovo, follow the regional road E-871/A2 and turn off towards Staro Nagorichane. Break here for refreshments and the church; fill your bottles at the fountain behind it.

Continue in the direction of Pelince and, three kilometres after Staro Nagorichane, turn right. From there it is 14 km to the megalithic observatory, uphill at an average gradient of 3.7% with a few flat stretches. The road passes through several villages where you can rest and refill.

If you want to camp, there is a well-equipped shelter before the village of Kokino with a fire pit, tables and benches, and a stream nearby; water is available in the village itself. You can also camp on the widened area used for parking beside the site.

Guided visits

The archaeoastronomical site is managed by the National Museum — Kumanovo, which provides expert guides for groups that announce their visit in advance. Arrangements are made directly with the museum.

What to bring

  • Proper shoes. The path is rock and loose ground, not a paved walkway.
  • Water. There is none on the hill. Fill up in the village.
  • Sun protection and a windbreak. The summit is exposed in every direction — that is rather the point of it.
  • Your headphones, and the audio guide already loaded before you leave signal.

When to come

Any clear day rewards the climb. For the alignments themselves, aim for a solstice or an equinox and arrive well before sunrise. Mid-May and the very end of July are when sunlight through the ritual marker lights the thrones. For the night sky — and this is a genuinely dark site — come on a moonless night in summer.

Common questions

Kokino, answered

The things people most often ask before they make the trip.

How do I get to Kokino from Skopje?

Kokino is about 90 km from Skopje and the drive takes roughly 1 to 2 hours. There is no direct public transport from Skopje, so most visitors drive, or book a private day trip. From Kumanovo you pass Staro Nagorichane and Dragomance, then take a narrow asphalt road to the foot of the hill.

How long does a visit to Kokino take?

Allow 1.5 to 2 hours at the site, plus about 15 to 20 minutes to walk up from the car park. A full day trip from Skopje, including travel, takes around 5 hours.

Is Kokino really NASA's fourth-oldest observatory?

No — that widely repeated ranking is a misunderstanding. In 2005 NASA’s Sun-Earth Connection Education Forum included Kokino in an educational listing of ancient observatories called Ancient Observatories: Timeless Knowledge. It was a mention, not a ranking, and NASA never published an order of oldest observatories. Kokino is genuinely one of the best-preserved Bronze Age archaeoastronomical sites in Europe, which is the more interesting fact.

What is Kokino and why is it important?

Kokino is a Bronze Age megalithic observatory and mountain sanctuary on the volcanic hill of Tatichev Kamen in North Macedonia, dating to roughly 1800 BC. Its builders cut platforms and stone markers into the rock to track the sunrises of the solstices and equinoxes and the extreme risings of the full moon, producing a lunar calendar on a 19-year cycle.

Is there an audio guide for Kokino?

Yes, and it is free. The original guide was a mobile app that went offline with the old website; the seven recordings are restored on this site and play in any phone browser. You can also download each part before you travel, because mobile signal on the ridge is unreliable. Open the audio guide.

When is the best time to visit Kokino?

Any clear day rewards the climb. To see the alignments themselves, come at a solstice or an equinox and arrive well before sunrise. Mid-May and the very end of July are when light through the ritual marker falls on the stone thrones. For stargazing, choose a moonless summer night — Kokino has a genuinely dark sky.

How much does it cost to visit Kokino?

A warden is on site from 9:00 to 17:00 and can supply printed material. Our private day trip from Skopje costs €119 in total for up to three people, with entrance tickets included; a traditional Macedonian lunch at Etno Selo Timčevski is optional at +€40 per person.

Is Kokino accessible for wheelchair users?

No. Reaching the platforms means a 15 to 20 minute walk up a rocky, uneven path with no surfaced route, so the site is not wheelchair accessible. Our day trip is also not suitable for children under 10 or travellers over 70.
